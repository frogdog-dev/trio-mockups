The core question

Who owns the model your workforce runs on?

Scale matters. Supply matters. Technology and managed services matter. But when one company provides the talent, the technology and the operating model, a more important question emerges.

How much control do you have over how your workforce runs?

The issue is bigger than features. It is about who owns the decisions, the data and the rules that shape your workforce, and whether you can change the model as your needs evolve.

The workforce is yours. The model should be too.

Signals the model may not be yours

  • Changing suppliers means changing technology, or the reverse.
  • Internal float pools, permanent hiring and contingent labor are managed as three separate strategies.
  • Not every source of supply is measured against the same expectations.
  • Rate reporting is available; total workforce economics are not.
  • Your program data lives somewhere you cannot fully govern.

Where control and governance sit today

How your score is calculated, four questions, 25 points each. Nothing else is weighted or inferred.

Each is worth 25 points because each is separately capable of stopping a change: concentration decides whether an alternative source of supply exists, supplier governance decides whether you can act on it, vendor neutrality decides whether the market actually competes for your roles, and rate governance decides who sets the price you pay. Full points means that component is governed by the health system; zero means it is decided or held outside it. Annual spend sizes the dollar figures only, it does not affect the score.

This index is a directional conversation tool, not an audit, actuarial finding, or financial projection. It reflects only the four inputs above and does not account for contract terms, quality or fill performance, clinical outcomes, or your local labor market. Scores and savings figures are illustrative and are not a guarantee of results. A Trio workforce analysis reviews your actual program data.

A vendor management system is the technology a hospital or health system uses to manage contingent labor end to end: requisitions, submissions, credentialing, scheduling, timecards, billing and supplier performance. A healthcare VMS models clinical realities such as licensure, privileging, unit-level scheduling and float pools rather than treating clinicians as generic contractors.

It means the platform you use to compare, award and govern work is not owned by a company competing to fill that work. Every supplier, including internal resources, is measured against the same expectations, and the health system decides where volume goes.
