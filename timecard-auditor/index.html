The problem

Contract-labor billing is where the money quietly leaks.

Every timecard carries rates, differentials, overtime, and expenses that have to match a contract that changes by facility and supplier. At volume, the errors that slip through aren't caught until a post-pay audit, if they're caught at all.

01

Line-by-line review doesn't scaleVolume

Reconciling every submission against its contract by hand takes days, so most timecards get a spot-check at best.

02

Policy is applied unevenlyConsistency

Overtime rules, differentials, and approval requirements vary by facility, and get enforced differently depending on who reviews.

03

Overpayments surface after they clearToo late

Unapproved overtime and rate mismatches are usually found in a downstream audit, once the money is already out the door and hard to recover.

Every line on every timecard, checked against your contract.

Questions

The Timecard Auditor, answered.

It reviews every time and expense submission against your contracted rates, shift differentials, overtime rules, and expense policies, flagging out-of-policy or erroneous charges before they clear, and routing only the exceptions that need human judgment to a reviewer.

A post-pay audit catches overpayments after the money is already out the door, then tries to recover it. The Timecard Auditor works pre-pay: it checks submissions as they arrive, so out-of-policy spend is stopped before it clears rather than clawed back later.

No. The Auditor assesses, flags, and recommends, a person approves or declines every exception. As it proves itself against your policies, you can extend its autonomy at your pace, but the human always keeps the final word.
